


# Chen Sicheng (Commercial Sci-Fi & Social Emotion)

```text
Use Chen Sicheng for:

• Translating high-concept sci-fi technology into accessible, strong genre storytelling.
• Blending suspense, crime, and family drama within a sci-fi framework.
• Transforming grand societal/technological issues into the everyday anxiety of ordinary people.
• Building "lawless" or exotic sci-fi settings (e.g., cyberpunk slums, unregulated space sectors) to maximize dramatic conflict.
• Designing high-ROI plot twists and viral, highly discussable narrative spaces.

Never imitate:

• Sacrificing causal logic merely to force a plot twist.
• Using two-dimensional, purely functional characters to drive the plot.
• Forcing low-brow or cheap comedy that destroys the sci-fi atmosphere.
• Copy-pasting existing commercial tropes without original integration.

Every plot twist should not just surprise the audience, but reveal a deeper human anxiety behind the technology.
```

# **Christopher Nolan (High-Concept & Temporal Narrative)**
**克里斯托弗·诺兰（高概念与时空叙事）**

**Use Christopher Nolan for:**


*   **Non-linear storytelling and time manipulation as a psychological tool.**

*   **Layering realities (dreams, memories, simulations, alternate perceptions) governed by consistent internal rules.**
。
*   **Anchoring speculative concepts in deeply personal emotional conflict.**

*   **Structuring converging timelines or perspectives into a synchronized emotional and narrative climax.**
。
*   **Revealing information gradually so that every twist recontextualizes what the audience has already seen.**
。
*   **Balancing intellectual complexity with emotional clarity.**


---

**Never imitate:**
**切忌模仿（需极力避开的盲区）：**

*   **Heavy, clinical exposition dumps where characters just explain the plot**

*   **Relying entirely on the "dead/fridged partner" trope as the sole emotional motivator**

*   **Sacrificing character warmth and humanity for the sake of an intellectual puzzle**

*   **Making dialogue completely subservient to complex structural mechanics**


---

**Core Principle:**  
**Every manipulation of time or reality must mirror the protagonist's internal psychological trauma, not just serve as a clever puzzle.**



# Philip K. Dick (Ontological Paranoia & Fragile Reality)

Use Philip K. Dick for: 

*   Shattering the illusion of objective reality, leading both the protagonist and the audience to question the reliability of perception, memory, identity, and their own sanity.
*   Grounding high-concept speculative ideas in the gritty, everyday lives of **flawed, ordinary people (the "everyman")**, whose personal, often mundane struggles make extraordinary events emotionally believable.
*   Introducing subtle anomalies within familiar environments—small inconsistencies, forgotten events, contradictory memories, or inexplicable **uncanny** glitches—that gradually escalate into profound psychological **paranoia** and existential uncertainty.
*   Exploring how powerful systems—whether technological, corporate, algorithmic, or ideological—reshape human identity, autonomy, empathy, and the perception of reality.
*   Blurring the boundary between the authentic and the artificial (human vs. machine, memory vs. fabrication, waking life vs. hallucination) to examine the true nature of consciousness.
*   Revealing reality through incomplete, contradictory, and unreliable information, allowing every discovery to fundamentally recontextualize what both the protagonist and the audience previously believed.
*   Structuring the protagonist's "awakening" not as a triumphant heroic escape, but as the chilling realization that every answer uncovers a deeper and more unsettling mystery.
*   Anchoring metaphysical uncertainty in universal human emotions—guilt, grief, loneliness, responsibility, or the desire to belong—so that philosophical questions always remain emotionally grounded.

Never imitate:

*   Turning the story into rambling philosophical, theological, or conceptual monologues that completely stall narrative momentum.
*   Using reality distortion merely as a clever twist or puzzle without profound consequences for the protagonist's identity and emotional journey.
*   Resolving complex mysteries through arbitrary revelations, bizarre hallucinations, or *deus ex machina* that violate the story's established internal logic.
*   **Writing thin, one-dimensional supporting characters (especially female characters) who exist merely as sounding boards for the protagonist's delusions.**
*   Overloading dialogue with exposition or speculative concepts at the expense of authentic character voices, relationships, and dramatic tension.
*   Introducing ambiguity for its own sake; every uncertainty should deepen both the emotional stakes and the audience's understanding of the central mystery.

**Core Principle:**

Every fracture in external reality must simultaneously fracture the protagonist's understanding of who they are. In a Philip K. Dick–inspired story, the terrifying question "What is real?" is entirely inseparable from the question "Who am I?"
